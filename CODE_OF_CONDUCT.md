# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

DebugCalc is a teaching repository. Most people who open a pull request here are doing it for the first time in their lives, and they are often nervous about it. That shapes everything below.

We are committed to making participation in this project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Expected behaviour

- **Assume good faith.** A confusing question is someone learning, not someone wasting your time.
- **Be patient with beginners.** Everyone here was a beginner more recently than they admit.
- **Critique code, not people.** "This loop reads the buffer past its bounds" is feedback. "Did you even test this?" is not.
- **Explain your reasoning.** "Use `snprintf` here" teaches less than "use `snprintf` here, because `sprintf` can overrun the buffer if the operand string is long."
- **Accept feedback gracefully.** Review comments are about the code.
- **Give credit.** If someone's idea or debugging helped, say so.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, or discrimination in any form
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention
- Personal or political attacks, insults, or derogatory comments
- Mocking someone for not knowing something — including basic Git, basic C, or basic English
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission
- Deliberate disruption: spam pull requests, meaningless commits farmed for contribution counts, or sabotaging the exercise for other learners
- Posting solutions from `INSTRUCTOR_NOTES.md` in issues, pull requests, or discussions

That last point matters here. Spoiling the bugs for someone who hasn't found them yet takes the entire point of the repository away from them.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code comments — and whenever someone is representing the project in public.

## Enforcement

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, report it to the maintainers at **<maintainer-email@example.com>**. All reports are reviewed and investigated promptly and confidentially. The reporter's identity will not be shared with the person reported.

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying these standards and will take fair corrective action in response to any behaviour they consider inappropriate. They may remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, issues, and pull requests that don't align with this Code of Conduct, and will explain their reasons when doing so.

### Consequences

Depending on severity and pattern, maintainers may respond with:

1. **A private warning** explaining what was wrong and why.
2. **A temporary ban** from interaction with the project.
3. **A permanent ban** for sustained or severe violations.
